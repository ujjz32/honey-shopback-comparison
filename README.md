# honey extension: how it works, what it actually saves, and when ShopBack is the better cashback option

Searching for the **Honey extension** usually means you want one of three things: automatic coupon testing, a way to avoid hunting for promo codes, or a browser tool that can help reduce the final checkout price.

Honey is built mainly around coupons. You install the browser extension, shop on a supported retailer’s website, and let Honey test available codes at checkout. If a working code is found, it applies the offer to the order. PayPal says Honey can test available coupon codes across more than 30,000 popular sites, although an eligible code is not guaranteed for every store or order.

There is another category of shopping extension worth understanding before you install anything: **cashback extensions**. Honey and ShopBack can appear similar because both work while you shop online, but they solve different problems.

- Honey focuses on finding and applying coupons.
- ShopBack focuses on activating cashback before you buy.
- Honey offers PayPal Rewards on eligible purchases.
- ShopBack tracks cashback through its browser extension, app, or website and lets eligible US users withdraw confirmed cashback through PayPal or ACH.

That distinction matters. A coupon can reduce the price immediately. Cashback usually appears later, after the retailer confirms the transaction and any return period has passed.

## What is the Honey extension?

The Honey extension is a free shopping tool from PayPal. It runs in supported desktop browsers and looks for coupon codes while you shop. The usual process is straightforward:

1. Install the extension.
2. Visit a supported store.
3. Add products to your cart.
4. Go to checkout.
5. Click the Honey prompt and let it test available codes.
6. Complete the order if the final price is acceptable.

Honey’s official site describes the extension as a three-step process: add it to your browser, shop normally, and let Honey test codes at checkout. It currently presents the service as working across more than 30,000 stores, including shopping, food delivery, travel, and other categories.

The important detail is that Honey does not invent a discount when the merchant has no usable offer. It searches its available coupon database and tests codes against the items in your cart. Some codes may be expired, limited to certain products, restricted to new customers, or blocked when another promotion is already active.

If Honey does not find a code that improves the order, that does not necessarily mean the extension is broken. It may simply mean the retailer has no compatible coupon for that cart.

## How Honey applies coupon codes

Honey normally becomes relevant near checkout rather than when you are first researching a product. Once you reach the retailer’s checkout page, the extension can offer to test coupon codes automatically. If a code works, Honey applies an eligible offer to the order.

A typical coupon test may fail for several ordinary reasons:

- The code applies only to full-price products.
- The cart contains excluded brands.
- The discount requires a minimum spend.
- The promotion is limited to new customers.
- The code cannot be combined with a sale price.
- The merchant has disabled the offer.
- The code is valid only in a specific country.
- Shipping, taxes, subscriptions, or gift cards are excluded.

This is why the final checkout total matters more than the coupon label. A “20% off” code is not automatically better than a smaller discount if the larger code excludes the product you want or removes free shipping.

The practical workflow is simple: keep the cart total visible, run Honey’s test, and check the final amount before paying. The coupon name is secondary. The actual total is the useful number.

## Honey extension versus Honey mobile app

The desktop extension and the mobile app are related, but they are not identical.

The browser extension follows you across supported shopping sites on a computer. The mobile app provides an in-app shopping environment where Honey can show offers, coupon codes, price information, and rewards. Honey says its mobile app is currently available to members in the United States and several other countries, including the United Kingdom, Canada, Australia, France, Germany, Italy, the Netherlands, India, and Spain.

Honey’s official help documentation describes the difference this way:

- The extension is designed for shopping across different sites in a desktop browser.
- The app brings participating stores and offers into one mobile shopping destination.
- Both can provide coupon access and eligible PayPal Rewards.
- The mobile experience can differ by retailer and market.

For US users, Honey also documents Amazon shopping support inside the mobile app’s in-app web view. Some features may vary depending on the platform, retailer, and account region, so it is better to treat Amazon support and mobile coupon behavior as feature-specific rather than assuming every store works the same way.

## Honey Rewards and PayPal Rewards

Honey is no longer only about coupon codes. Eligible purchases can also earn PayPal Rewards points when the relevant offer is activated and the purchase meets the program terms.

PayPal states that Rewards points can be earned through eligible offers on PayPal, the PayPal app, and PayPal Honey. Points may be based on the eligible item subtotal, while taxes, shipping, and other charges may not count.

Honey’s help documentation says US members can connect Honey and PayPal accounts to redeem points at PayPal checkout. It also states that, after earning 1,000 points, members may redeem them for a $10 gift card from participating stores, subject to the applicable program rules and available redemption options.

That means the reward side of Honey should be viewed as an additional benefit, not a guaranteed percentage discount on every order. Rewards are offer-dependent. A store may provide a coupon but no points, points but no coupon, or neither.

Before relying on rewards for a large purchase, check:

- Whether the store is listed as eligible.
- Whether the offer must be activated manually.
- Which products qualify.
- Whether points are calculated before or after discounts.
- Whether returns or refunds reverse the points.
- Which redemption options are available in your account.

PayPal’s Rewards terms also allow points or awards to be removed when an underlying order is returned, refunded, charged back, or found to violate the program rules.

## What Honey can and cannot do

Honey is useful when your main problem is coupon research. It can save time by testing multiple codes instead of making you open several coupon pages and paste them one by one.

It is less useful when your main goal is to earn cashback on purchases where no coupon is available. Honey may offer rewards on selected transactions, but the availability and amount depend on the store and offer.

Here is the practical breakdown:

| Shopping goal | Honey extension fit |
| --- | --- |
| Automatically test coupon codes | Strong fit |
| Check whether a promo code works | Strong fit |
| Track price drops | Useful through Droplist |
| Compare Amazon seller prices | Useful in supported experiences |
| Earn rewards on every purchase | Not guaranteed |
| Activate retailer cashback before checkout | Not its main purpose |
| Withdraw cashback to PayPal or a bank account | Depends on the reward program, not ordinary coupons |

Honey also includes Droplist, a price-tracking feature. You can add an item and receive a notification if Honey detects a price drop at the relevant store. The feature is helpful when you are not ready to buy immediately, but it should not be interpreted as a guarantee that the lowest possible market price has been found.

## Honey versus ShopBack

The link associated with this article resolves to ShopBack US, a cashback platform with a browser extension, website, and mobile app. ShopBack is therefore best understood as a cashback alternative or companion to a coupon extension, not as the Honey extension itself.

The two services differ at the point where the saving happens.

### Honey

Honey generally works at checkout by testing coupon codes. The benefit is immediate when a code applies.

### ShopBack

ShopBack works by activating cashback before checkout. The browser extension can notify you when a participating store is eligible, and you activate the offer before completing the order. The cashback is then tracked and becomes available after the merchant confirms the transaction.

A simple example makes the difference clearer:

- You buy a $100 item.
- Honey finds a valid 15% coupon.
- Your checkout total may drop to $85 before tax and shipping.
- ShopBack may separately offer cashback on the qualifying purchase.
- ShopBack cashback is not normally an instant discount at checkout. It appears later after tracking and merchant confirmation.

Whether both benefits can be combined depends on the merchant’s rules. ShopBack warns that cashback may fail to track when an unapproved coupon code is used, when another cashback link is clicked, or when the shopping session changes in a way that breaks attribution.

In other words, do not assume that every coupon and every cashback offer will stack. Read the retailer’s exclusions and follow the activation instructions shown by the platform.

## How to use the ShopBack browser extension

If you decide that cashback is more useful than coupon testing for a particular order, the process is usually:

1. Create or sign in to a ShopBack account.
2. Install the ShopBack browser extension on a supported browser.
3. Visit a participating retailer.
4. Wait for the cashback notification.
5. Click **Activate Cashback**.
6. Complete the purchase in the same tracked browser session.
7. Check the cashback status in your ShopBack account.

ShopBack’s support documentation specifically recommends installing the extension, pinning it to the browser toolbar, and activating cashback when the extension displays the retailer’s offer.

The same-session requirement is easy to overlook. Opening another cashback site, clicking a different affiliate link, switching devices, or using a restricted coupon can interfere with tracking. Returns, cancellations, and partial refunds can also reverse or reduce the cashback.

For a clean transaction, it helps to do the shopping in this order:

- Decide what you want to buy first.
- Check the retailer’s current price and shipping terms.
- Review the available cashback rate.
- Activate cashback.
- Avoid opening unrelated cashback links afterward.
- Complete checkout without changing devices.
- Save the order confirmation until the cashback is confirmed.

You can then use a coupon if the retailer and ShopBack allow it. If the cashback terms exclude outside promo codes, using an unapproved code may cost more than it saves.

## Is the Honey extension free?

Yes. Honey presents the browser extension as free to install and use. Its business model is based on commissions from participating merchant partners when members use Honey to find savings or activate eligible PayPal Rewards. Honey says it shares part of those earnings through its rewards program and does not sell user data as part of that model.

“Free” does not mean the extension has no permissions. Like other shopping extensions, Honey needs access to relevant shopping pages so it can identify the retailer, detect checkout fields, and apply a code when you request it. Honey explains that it reads pages on shopping websites to find coupons and changes page information when applying coupon codes at the user’s request.

That permission model is normal for a coupon extension, but it is still worth reviewing before installation. Use the official browser extension listing or the official PayPal Honey website, keep the browser updated, and remove the extension if you no longer use it.

## Is Honey worth installing?

Honey is worth considering if you regularly shop online and often forget to search for promo codes. The biggest benefit is convenience. It can test codes in the background and tell you when no compatible offer is available.

It is a weaker fit if you expect every order to receive a discount or if you mainly want predictable cashback. Coupon availability is not guaranteed, and the most visible code is not necessarily the most valuable result for your particular cart.

A reasonable decision rule is:

- Use Honey when you want coupon testing at checkout.
- Use ShopBack when you want cashback tracking and later withdrawal.
- Check both only when the retailer’s rules clearly allow the benefits to work together.
- Compare the final price, shipping cost, taxes, and confirmed reward value instead of focusing on the advertised percentage.

For occasional shopping, Honey’s automation may be enough. For people who book travel, buy from major retailers, or make frequent online purchases, a cashback extension can be more useful because the savings are not limited to stores with active coupon codes.

## Common Honey extension questions

### Does Honey guarantee a coupon?

No. Honey searches and tests available codes, but it cannot guarantee that a valid code exists for every store, cart, or product. A retailer may have no eligible promotion, or the available codes may exclude the items in your order.

### Does Honey work on every website?

No. Honey supports a large number of stores, but coverage and available features vary. The extension may appear on a site without finding a usable code.

### Can Honey and ShopBack be used together?

Sometimes, but do not assume they will stack. ShopBack says cashback can fail to track when unapproved coupon codes or other cashback links interfere with attribution. Check the specific merchant terms before combining them.

### Can Honey track price drops?

Yes. Honey’s Droplist can notify you when it detects a price drop for a saved item at a supported store.

### Does Honey work on mobile?

Honey offers a mobile app in the United States and other listed markets. The desktop browser extension and mobile app work differently, so a feature available in one may not appear in the other.

### How does ShopBack pay cashback?

For US users, ShopBack says eligible confirmed cashback can be withdrawn through PayPal or ACH after the applicable minimum is reached. Cashback must normally be tracked and confirmed by the merchant first.
